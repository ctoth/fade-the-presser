FOMC communications | The Warsh Fed

Read the minutes. Fade the presser.

The Chair explains the decision on decision day. Three weeks later the Committee’s minutes explain it differently. This ledger measures the gap after every meeting — automatically, for as long as the gap exists.

Updated when minutes are published · scored by machine, quarrels welcome

Method

A rate decision needs a majority of the twelve-member Committee; the Chair holds a single vote. The unit of account here is therefore the claim pair: the Chair’s characterisation of a point set against the minutes’ record of the same point, scored 0–100 by how far the two diverge (0: same substance; 50: a clear difference of emphasis or attribution; 100: contradiction). Pairs are chosen for substance, not for disagreement, so an uneventful meeting can score as uneventful. Minutes vocabulary is weighted the way Fed-watchers weight it: “most” is consensus, “many” a strong majority, “various” is far short of either.

Each meeting is scored in five independent runs. Every quoted fragment in every run is checked verbatim against the Federal Reserve source text; a run with any unverifiable quote is discarded. The published basis is the mean across surviving runs, shown with its range, and the pairs displayed come from the run closest to that mean. A single run is an opinion; the range is the measurement.
