Read the minutes. Fade the presser.

The Chair explains the decision on decision day. Three weeks later the Committee’s minutes explain it differently. This ledger measures the gap after every meeting — automatically, for as long as the gap exists.

Updated when minutes are published · scored by machine, quarrels welcome

Method

It takes seven votes to change policy; the Chair casts one. So the unit of account here is the claim pair: the Chair’s characterisation of the decision set against the minutes’ record of it, scored 0–100 by how far the two diverge. The meeting’s basis is the mean across claims. Minutes vocabulary is weighted the way Fed-watchers weight it: “most” is consensus, “many” a strong majority, “various” is far short of either.
